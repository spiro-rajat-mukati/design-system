A two-minute orientation: what this is, what it gives you, and how to get what isn't here yet.

What is a design system, in plain language

A design system is a shared set of building blocks — colours, typography, spacing, buttons, forms — that designers and developers both use. Instead of designing and coding a button from scratch each time, the team picks "the button" from the library. The product looks consistent, ships faster, and accessibility / dark mode / mobile-friendliness come for free.

What this system gives your team

  • Faster shipping. 16 production-ready components mean a typical form-heavy screen drops from a week of design + a week of build to a few days of each.
  • Consistency across products. Whatever Kijani ships next will look like it belongs in the same product as everything else. No drift between teams.
  • Dark mode, touch-friendly, accessible, RTL-ready by default. These aren't follow-up tasks — they're baked in to every component.
  • One source of truth. When the brand colour changes, it changes in code and in Figma in one motion. No "the designs say one blue and the product ships another" problems.
  • Lower review overhead. Less debate about "what should this look like" — most visual decisions are already made. Reviews focus on UX and content, not pixel-level details.

How to request something

Most asks fall into one of three buckets — they each have a different lead time:

  • Token tweak (e.g. "the brand blue feels a bit dark"): same day to a week. File an issue or post in #design-system.
  • New variant of an existing component (e.g. "we need an extra-small destructive button"): 1-2 weeks. RFC required, both leads review.
  • New component (e.g. "we need a date picker"): 3-6 weeks. Full RFC with use cases, design exploration, accessibility considerations.

If you're not sure which bucket applies, ask. The leads will route it.

Who owns this

The design system is owned by the design and engineering leads jointly. They're the approvers on token changes, new components, and breaking changes. Everyone on the team can propose changes; both leads have to sign off.
